# Official FAQs: Broker Charges, Disputed Debits, and Investor Rights

### Q1: Can a stock broker debit charges without sending a prior intimation or invoice?
**Answer**: No. Under the SEBI Master Circular for Stock Brokers, brokers are strictly required to issue itemized contract notes within 24 hours of trade execution and provide monthly/quarterly ledger statements. Any ad-hoc fee, maintenance debit, or penalty must correspond to the schedule of charges agreed upon during client account onboarding. Any unexplained debit entry (e.g. ₹2,500 under 'sundry debit' or 'admin fee') is legally contestable.

### Q2: What should an investor do if they notice an unauthorized deduction of ₹2,500 in their ledger?
**Answer**:
1. First, check the contract notes and the tariff sheet signed at account opening.
2. Lodge an official written complaint with the broker's compliance department or grievance officer via email, asking for an itemized invoice and regulatory basis.
3. If the broker fails to provide justification within 15-21 days, file a complaint on the SEBI SCORES 2.0 portal (scores.sebi.gov.in) with the ledger statement and correspondence as evidence.

### Q3: What is the maximum brokerage a broker can charge?
**Answer**: Under SEBI norms, the maximum brokerage chargeable by a stock broker for cash market transactions shall not exceed 2.5% of the contract price of the transaction (exclusive of statutory levies like STT, GST, Stamp Duty, and exchange turnover charges).

### Q4: What constitutes evidence of an unauthorized trade?
**Answer**:
- Absence of pre-trade voice recording on telephone lines.
- Absence of client login IP/device logs or OTP 2-factor authentication.
- Absence of signed physical order execution slip.
If the broker cannot produce these records, the trade is deemed unauthorized under exchange regulations.
